How El Niño reaches Liberia

Usually drier and hotter

El Niño tends to weaken the West African monsoon, bringing longer dry spells and higher temperatures to coastal West Africa.

But not always

In 2023–24, El Niño plus a positive Indian Ocean Dipole kept rain falling unusually long on Liberia's coast. A positive dipole is active again now, so floods remain possible.

The Atlantic decides a lot

Gulf of Guinea sea temperatures shape Liberia's rainfall as much as the Pacific. AGRHYMET noted cooling there this season, which limits coastal rain.

Preparedness

Early actions for each hazard and the agencies proposed to lead them.

Draft for EPA review. Lead agencies are proposed and need confirmation. More

Draft for EPA review: suggested actions based on standard El Niño early-action practice in West Africa. Lead agencies are proposed and need confirmation with each institution.

Dryness & drought

Likely impacts: reduced lowland rice and cassava yields, falling well and creek levels, more bush fires, higher food prices.

  • Promote early-maturing and drought-tolerant seed for 2027 planting (MOA).
  • Map and protect community water points; plan water trucking for Monrovia's dry months (LWSC).
  • Bush-fire awareness and burning controls in Bong, Nimba and Lofa (EPA, FDA).
  • Monitor river levels for hydropower and water supply (Hydrological Service).

Flooding

Likely impacts: flash floods in Greater Monrovia, displacement in low-lying communities, waterborne disease.

  • Clear drains and waterways in flood-prone communities (MCC, EPA).
  • Pre-position relief stocks for at-risk communities (NDMA).
  • Issue flood alerts from LiMet heavy-rain forecasts (LiMet, NDMA).

Heat

Likely impacts: heat stress for outdoor workers, children and the elderly; higher electricity demand; livestock stress.

  • Public heat-health messaging on hydration and shade (MOH, NPHIL).
  • Adjust school and market hours during heat peaks (MOE, local authorities).

Windstorm

Likely impacts: roofs torn off and houses destroyed, displacement to host families, damaged schools and hand pumps. Recorded events in 2024 and 2026 both struck in March, in Nimba, Lofa, Bong and Gbarpolu.

  • Before March: promote roof tie-downs (tie wire, strapping) and safe-shelter awareness in Nimba, Lofa, Bong and Gbarpolu (NDMA, Red Cross).
  • Check school and clinic roofs before the transition season (MOE, MOH).
  • Pre-position zinc, nails, timber and tarpaulins with county authorities (NDMA).
  • Relay LiMet severe-thunderstorm warnings through community radio (LiMet).

Health

Likely impacts: cholera and diarrhoea after floods or with water shortages; changing malaria patterns.

  • Strengthen disease surveillance in hazard-affected counties (NPHIL).
  • Stock oral rehydration salts and water treatment supplies (MOH).

About & Data

What this platform is, how levels are set, and where every figure comes from.

About this platform

Hosted by the Environmental Protection Agency of Liberia to monitor the 2026–27 El Niño and coordinate preparedness. Phase 1 covers monitoring, county hazard mapping, preparedness and bulletins. District population is now included; farmland, infrastructure, vulnerability and sector impact estimates follow in later phases.

Definitions

Niño 3.4

Sea temperature anomaly in the central-east equatorial Pacific, the main region used to track El Niño.

RONI

NOAA's official index since Feb 2026: Niño 3.4 minus tropical-average warming, as a 3-month mean. El Niño is +0.5 °C or more. Weak 0.5–0.9, moderate 1.0–1.4, strong 1.5–1.9, very strong 2.0+.

Warning / Watch / Low

Dryness: share of cropland under drought stress (Warning 25% or more, Watch 10–25%). Flood exposure: share of people in the 1-in-100-year river flood zone (Warning 10% or more, Watch 3–10%). Heat: Warning when a regional bulletin flags the county now, Watch when signals point to elevated risk, Low when there is no current signal.

Data sources

  • El Niño indices and outlooks: NOAA Climate Prediction Center data files (RONI table, weekly and monthly Niño indices, official ENSO probability and strength tables, monthly ENSO discussion), checked daily by a scheduled task.
  • Liberia hazards: NOAA CPC / FEWS NET Africa and Global Weather Hazards (Oct–Nov 2026); AGRHYMET seasonal update (30 Jul 2026).
  • Boundaries: LISGIS 2007 district (136) and clan (816) boundaries, edition 14; counties are formed from the districts. Clan names are pending the clan attribute table.
  • Population: LISGIS 2022 Liberia Population and Housing Census, final results (June 2023), Appendix B district tables; households from the 2008 census. The 2022 census lists 172 districts, the 2007 map 136. Where a 2007 district keeps its name, it gets its 2022 figure; people in new 2022 districts (for example Montserrado's townships, four new Lofa districts, Tehr, Penicess, Kabah Administrative) are shared among their county's 2007 districts using WorldPop's settlement pattern, so county totals are exact and some district figures are estimates. Three table errors were corrected: Gounwolaila (17,986) belongs to Gbarpolu, not Grand Cape Mount or Margibi; Owensgrove (14,422) is missing from the Grand Bassa table and is the county total minus the listed districts; Sinoe's listed districts fall 17,478 short of its total.
  • Ocean and rain monitor: NASA GIBS imagery of GHRSST Level 4 MUR sea-surface temperature anomaly and GPM IMERG daily precipitation rate, last 30 days, with values read from NASA's published colour tables; NASA imagery is free to reuse. Rebuilt daily by a scheduled task.
  • Crop stress map: FAO ASIS 1 km Agricultural Stress Index (dekadal, Season 1, cropland) and Historic Agricultural Drought Frequency (30% threshold, 1984–2025), from FAO's map service; district and clan values are estimated from the map.
  • Drought and crops: FAO GIEWS Agricultural Stress Index System (ASIS), Liberia county files for the Agricultural Stress Index, Vegetation Health Index and satellite rainfall estimate (FEWS NET RFE), refreshed automatically when FAO updates.
  • Windstorm events: NDMA briefing via allAfrica (Mar 2024); IFRC DREF Operation MDRLR010 (Apr 2026); allAfrica (Apr 2026).
  • Wind: Global Wind Atlas 3 (DTU, with the World Bank / ESMAP), mean wind speed and power density at 10, 50 and 100 m, 250 m grid, CC BY 4.0; averaged over each county, district and clan.
  • Flood exposure: JRC Copernicus Emergency Management Service Global River Flood Hazard Maps, version 2.1 (Baugh et al. 2024), flood depth for 1-in-10, 1-in-100 and 1-in-500-year river floods at about 90 m, free to reuse; combined with WorldPop 2025 constrained population at 100 m (CC BY 4.0), scaled to 2022 census district totals, to count people and area in the flood zone of every county, district and clan. River floods only; coastal surge and urban drainage flooding are not included.
  • Updates: NOAA's monthly ENSO discussion comes out on the second Thursday of each month; the hazards outlook weekly. The platform updates itself automatically: NASA maps daily, NOAA El Niño data daily, FAO drought data weekly.
